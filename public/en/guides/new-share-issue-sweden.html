Guide

New share issue, step by step

From the board's proposal to registration with Bolagsverket, with a reference to chapter 13 of the Swedish Companies Act for every step.

Last reviewed 17 November 2026. This guide describes a new issue of shares in a Swedish private limited company that is not CSD-registered. It is a summary, not legal advice.

Overview

  1. The board drafts a proposal
  2. Decision on the new issue
  3. Subscription on a subscription list
  4. Payment
  5. Allotment and share register
  6. Registration with Bolagsverket

1. Proposal

If the general meeting is to decide on a new issue, the board drafts a proposal for the decision (chapter 13, section 3 of the Companies Act). The decision must state, among other things, how payment is made, for example whether shares may be paid for in kind (chapter 13, section 5).

2. Who decides?

  • The general meeting decides on the issue, on the board's proposal.
  • The board under an authorisation: the meeting can authorise the board to decide on a new issue, as far as it can be done without changing the articles (chapter 13, section 35).
  • The board subject to later approval: the board can decide on condition that the general meeting approves the decision afterwards (chapter 13, section 31). Without approval, the decision may not be registered (chapter 13, section 34).

Pre-emption rights

The main rule is that existing shareholders have a preferential right to the new shares in proportion to the shares they already own (chapter 13, section 1).

3. Subscription

New shares are subscribed for on a subscription list that contains the issue decision (chapter 13, section 13). Subscription lists can be signed with BankID in Custora.

4. Payment

Subscribed shares are paid in money or, if the issue decision allows it, in kind (apportegendom) (chapter 13, section 20). Property paid in kind must be useful to the company's business, and a promise to do work or provide services does not count (chapter 2, section 6).

5. Allotment and share register

Allotted shares must be entered in the share register straight away (chapter 13, section 18). If the board decided subject to the meeting's approval, the shares may not be entered until the meeting has approved the decision (chapter 13, section 32). See what the law requires of the share register.

6. Registration with Bolagsverket

The board must file the decision for registration in the companies register within six months of the decision (chapter 13, section 27). Registration fixes the increase in share capital (chapter 13, section 29).

In Custora

You record the issue once, with date, subscribers and share numbers. The register, the holdings and the group structure update on their own, and minutes and subscription lists are signed with BankID and filed with the company. More on the digital share register.

Sources

  • Swedish Companies Act (2005:551): ch. 2 s. 6; ch. 13 ss. 1, 3, 5, 13, 18, 20, 27, 29, 31, 32, 34, 35 (Swedish)

Frequently asked questions

How long do you have to register a new issue?
The board must file the decision for registration within six months of the decision (chapter 13, section 27).
When are the new shares entered in the register?
Allotted shares are entered straight away (chapter 13, section 18). If the board decided subject to the meeting's approval, they wait until the meeting has approved it (chapter 13, section 32).
Can shares be paid for with something other than money?
Yes, in kind if the issue decision allows it (chapter 13, section 20). Work or services do not count (chapter 2, section 6).
