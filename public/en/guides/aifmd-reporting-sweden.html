AIFMD reporting to Finansinspektionen: how it works

A walkthrough for Swedish AIF managers: who has to report to Finansinspektionen, how often, which templates apply and how the report is submitted.

Last reviewed 13 November 2026. This guide is a summary and does not replace Finansinspektionen's instructions. Always check the current rules with FI.

Who has to report?

According to Finansinspektionen (FI), both authorised and registered Swedish AIF managers must report to FI. Non-EEA AIF managers that market a fund in Sweden must report too. Reporting covers the manager and each alternative investment fund.

Basis: chapter 2 section 4 and chapter 13 sections 2–4 of the Swedish Alternative Investment Fund Managers Act (2013:561, LAIF), and articles 3 and 24 of the AIFM Directive.

Which templates apply?

ManagerTemplates
Registered AIF manager24(1)
Authorised AIF manager24(1) and 24(2)
Fund using leverage on a substantial basisAlso 24(4)

The templates are in Annex IV of Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) No 231/2013. The first part covers the manager and the others cover the funds.

How often?

How often a manager reports depends mainly on its assets under management. The rules are in Article 110 of Regulation 231/2013:

Assets under management or fund typeFrequency
Above the EUR 100 million or 500 million threshold but no more than EUR 1 billionHalf-yearly
Above EUR 1 billionQuarterly
A fund above EUR 500 million at a manager that reports half-yearlyQuarterly for that fund
An unleveraged fund that invests in non-listed companies to acquire controlAnnually

FI can require more frequent reporting. A manager that wants to report less often must, according to FI, notify rapportering@fi.se.

When is it due?

  • The reference date is always the last day of a quarter.
  • The report must reach FI no later than one month after the end of the period.
  • For a fund of funds the period can be extended by 15 days.
  • First reporting takes place one calendar quarter after the manager was authorised.

What the report contains

The report covers the manager and each fund. Two points FI highlights:

  • Leverage is expressed as the ratio between the fund's exposure and its net asset value (NAV). A fund without leverage reports 100%.
  • The risk measures Net Equity Delta, Net DV01 and Net CS01 are mandatory for funds reporting under 24(2).

Fields marked with an asterisk are fields FI may request. FI's position is that managers report the fields that are available and relevant.

How is it submitted?

All ongoing reporting for funds and AIF managers goes through Fidac, FI's reporting system, which you reach through FI's e-services after registering a user. FI refers to ESMA's XML schemas, sample files and validation rules.

Where it usually gets stuck

The hard part is rarely the file itself. It is the input: which funds, investors and holdings existed on the reference date. When that sits in different spreadsheets it takes time to pull together every period, and two reports can easily end up built on different versions.

In Custora funds, investors, commitments and the company structure sit in one place, with every change dated. That makes it easier to pull the input for each reference date. Read more about Custora for fund managers.

Frequently asked questions

Do registered AIF managers have to report?
Yes. Both authorised and registered Swedish AIF managers report to FI. Registered managers use template 24(1).
When is the report due?
No later than one month after the end of the period. For a fund of funds the period can be extended by 15 days.
Where is the report submitted?
In Fidac, Finansinspektionen's reporting system, through FI's e-services.
