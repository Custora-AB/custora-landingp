Why it gets messy

It is common for each property to sit in its own limited company, often with a holding company in between. Under the Swedish Companies Act each company needs its own share register, a board that keeps minutes and an annual general meeting. With 40 companies that is 40 registers to keep current, at least 40 sets of AGM minutes a year and a group structure that changes every time a property is bought or sold.

Each company's board is responsible for its share register (Companies Act, chapter 5, section 7). In practice the work is done centrally, often in spreadsheets that only one or two people really know.

When a property is sold

When a property company is sold you record the transfer once. The register updates, the company leaves the group tree and the history is kept. The transfer agreement and the board minutes can be signed with BankID and go into the company's data room.

The same goes when you buy or form companies: the new company has a register from day one and shows up in the structure straight away.

Frequently asked questions

Can a share register be kept digitally?
Yes. Chapter 5, section 2 of the Swedish Companies Act says the register shall be kept by automated processing. Companies that are not CSD-registered may also keep it in a bound book or a secure loose-leaf or card system. A digital register follows the main rule.
Who is responsible for the share register?
In companies that are not CSD-registered, the board is responsible for keeping, storing and making the register available (chapter 5, section 7). The company is the data controller for the personal data in it (chapter 5, section 4).
Does Custora replace our property or finance system?
No. Custora holds ownership and governance: registers, structure, decisions and signed documents. Rent, accounting and property management stay in your current systems.
