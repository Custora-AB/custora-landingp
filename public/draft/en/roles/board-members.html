What the Companies Act puts on the board

  • The register: in companies that are not CSD-registered, the board is responsible for keeping, storing and making the share register available (chapter 5, section 7).
  • Changes: must be entered in the register straight away and dated (chapter 5, section 9).
  • Access: the register must be available to anyone who wants to see it. If it is kept digitally, the company must be able to show a current printout (chapter 5, section 10).
  • Minutes: every board decision is recorded, and board minutes are kept in numbered order and stored securely (chapter 8, sections 24 and 26).

The responsibility stays with the board even when someone else does the practical work, such as a finance lead, a fund manager or a firm. In Custora the register is built from the company's transactions and the minutes are kept in order per company, so what the board answers for sits in one place.

Frequently asked questions

Who is responsible for the share register?
In companies that are not CSD-registered, the board is responsible for keeping, storing and making the register available (chapter 5, section 7). The company is the data controller for the personal data in it (chapter 5, section 4).
Who can see the share register?
In companies that are not CSD-registered, the register must be available at the company to anyone who wants to see it. If it is kept digitally, the company must be able to show a current printout to anyone who asks (chapter 5, section 10).
Who signs board minutes?
The person who took the minutes signs them and the chair checks them, unless the chair took them. If the board has several members, one more member chosen by the board also checks them (chapter 8, section 24).
