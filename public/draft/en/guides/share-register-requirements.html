Share register: what the law requires

Contents, responsibility, restrictions, changes and retention of the share register (aktiebok) under the Swedish Companies Act.

Last reviewed 17 November 2026. This guide covers Swedish limited companies that are not CSD-registered (avstämningsbolag). It is a summary, not legal advice.

What is a share register?

The share register (aktiebok) is the company's list of its shares and who owns them. Every Swedish limited company must have one. In a company that is not CSD-registered, the company keeps it itself, and the board is responsible for keeping, storing and making it available (chapter 5, section 7 of the Companies Act).

What must the register contain?

Under chapter 5, section 5 the register must show:

  • the number of each share
  • each shareholder's name, personal or company ID number and postal address
  • the class of each share, if the company has more than one
  • whether share certificates have been issued
  • whether the share is subject to a restriction in the articles

Shares are listed in numerical order.

Restrictions that must show

  • Consent clause (samtyckesförbehåll): shares may only be transferred with the company's consent (chapter 4, section 8).
  • Right of first offer (förköpsförbehåll): a shareholder or other person must be offered the share before it is transferred to a new owner (chapter 4, section 18).
  • Pre-emption clause (hembudsförbehåll): a shareholder or other person may buy out a share that has passed to a new owner (chapter 4, section 27).

When the register must be updated

Changes must be entered straight away and dated (chapter 5, section 9). In a new issue, allotted shares must be entered in the register straight away (chapter 13, section 18). If the board decided the issue subject to the general meeting's approval, the new shares may not be entered until the meeting has approved it (chapter 13, section 32). See the guide to new share issues.

Can it be kept digitally?

Yes. The main rule in chapter 5, section 2 is that the register is kept by automated processing. Companies that are not CSD-registered may also keep it in a bound book or a secure loose-leaf or card system.

Who can see it, and how long is it kept?

  • The register must be available at the company to anyone who wants to see it. If it is kept digitally, the company must be able to show a current printout (chapter 5, section 10).
  • It must be kept for as long as the company exists and for at least ten years after it is dissolved (chapter 5, section 3).
  • The company is the data controller for the personal data in it (chapter 5, section 4).

Share certificates

The company must issue a share certificate if a shareholder asks for one (chapter 6, section 1). If it has, the register must say so.

Where it usually goes wrong

With many companies and registers in Excel, it is easy for a transfer never to be recorded, for share numbers not to add up, or for two versions to exist at once. In Custora the register is built from the company's transactions, with a date on every change. More on the digital share register.

Sources

  • Swedish Companies Act (2005:551): ch. 4 ss. 8, 18, 27; ch. 5 ss. 2–10; ch. 6 s. 1; ch. 13 ss. 18, 32 (Swedish)

Frequently asked questions

Can a share register be kept digitally?
Yes. Chapter 5, section 2 of the Swedish Companies Act says the register shall be kept by automated processing. Companies that are not CSD-registered may also keep it in a bound book or a secure loose-leaf or card system. A digital register follows the main rule.
Who is responsible for the share register?
In companies that are not CSD-registered, the board is responsible for keeping, storing and making the register available (chapter 5, section 7). The company is the data controller for the personal data in it (chapter 5, section 4).
How long must the register be kept?
For as long as the company exists and for at least ten years after it is dissolved (chapter 5, section 3).
